# Hispanic Health Paradox Analysis

This project looks at the Hispanic Health Paradox using data from the NYC Community Health Survey. The goal was to look at different health and socioeconomic factors and see whether the patterns in the data supported the Hispanic Health Paradox.

## What I Analyzed

I focused on differences between Hispanic and non-Hispanic groups in areas such as poverty, healthcare access, and asthma diagnosis. I also looked at other demographic and socioeconomic factors that could help explain these differences.

## Methods

I used R to clean and analyze the survey data. Since the data came from a complex survey, I used survey-weighted statistical models to make sure the analysis properly accounted for the survey design.

Some of the methods used in this project include:

- Data cleaning and recoding
- Exploratory data analysis
- Survey-weighted regression
- Logistic regression
- Ordinal logistic regression
- Data visualization

## Main Findings

The data showed that Hispanics had a higher proportion of individuals in lower-income groups and also had the highest raw proportion of delayed medical care due to cost. However, after accounting for other demographic and socioeconomic factors, being Hispanic was not a statistically significant predictor of delayed medical care.

For asthma, the results also did not show a statistically significant difference between Hispanics and White non-Hispanics in age at first asthma diagnosis after adjusting for other factors.

Overall, the project showed how looking at the raw data alone can tell a different story than a statistical model that accounts for other factors.
